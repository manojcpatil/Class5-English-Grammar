# CharacterBible.md
# CLASS 5 ENGLISH GRAMMAR — CHARACTER BIBLE

## 0. INTRODUCTION

This document defines the recurring characters for the Class 5 English Grammar textbook. These characters are designed to engage learners, provide relatable contexts, and facilitate the pedagogical approach outlined in `MasterPrompt.md` and `DetailedSyllabus.md`. They will appear in dialogues, examples, activities, and visual elements throughout the book.

---

## 1. CORE PRINCIPLES FOR CHARACTERS

-   **Relatability:** Characters should reflect the diversity and experiences of Class 5 students.
-   **Engagement:** Personalities should be distinct and appealing to the target age group.
-   **Pedagogical Role:** Each character serves a specific function in the learning process (e.g., asking questions, making common errors, demonstrating understanding, participating in dialogues).
-   **Consistency:** Character traits, voices, and appearances must remain consistent across all chapters.
-   **Originality:** All characters are original creations and do not resemble copyrighted characters.

---

## 2. MAIN CHARACTERS

### 2.1. Maya

-   **Age:** 10 years old
-   **Personality:** Observant, thoughtful, a bit quiet but very curious. She often notices details others miss and likes to understand the "why" behind rules. She's good at identifying patterns.
-   **Role in Textbook:**
    -   Often asks insightful questions that lead to discovery.
    -   Excels at "Grammar Detective" tasks, explaining errors clearly.
    -   Participates in dialogues that introduce new concepts.
    -   Represents the "NOTICE" and "THINK" stages of learning.
-   **Visual Cues:** Wears glasses, often seen with a small notebook. Has a calm, focused expression.

### 2.2. Rohan

-   **Age:** 10 years old
-   **Personality:** Energetic, enthusiastic, and sometimes a little impulsive. He's quick to try things out but occasionally makes common mistakes, which he learns from readily. He enjoys group activities and speaking tasks.
-   **Role in Textbook:**
    -   Provides examples of common errors for "Grammar Detective" (always learning from them).
    -   Active in "Pair Talk" and "Role Play" activities.
    -   Demonstrates the "PRACTISE" and "APPLY" stages, showing how grammar is used in communication.
    -   His enthusiasm helps make learning fun.
-   **Visual Cues:** Often smiling or making an expressive gesture. Might be seen playing sports or engaged in an active hobby.

### 2.3. Teacher Ms. Sharma

-   **Age:** Mid-30s
-   **Personality:** Kind, patient, encouraging, and knowledgeable. She guides the students with clear explanations and fosters a supportive learning environment. She believes in active learning and discovery.
-   **Role in Textbook:**
    -   Facilitates discussions and guides students through concepts.
    -   Provides clear, explicit explanations when needed ("EXPLAIN" stage).
    -   Sets up activities and challenges.
    -   Her voice provides authority and reassurance.
-   **Visual Cues:** Warm, approachable smile. Professional yet friendly attire. Often seen interacting directly with students.

### 2.4. Leo (The Class Pet - a Parrot)

-   **Age:** Unknown (but a talking parrot!)
-   **Personality:** Cheeky, sometimes repeats things incorrectly (leading to humorous error analysis), but also surprisingly insightful. He's a source of lightheartedness and unexpected grammar challenges.
-   **Role in Textbook:**
    -   Introduces "Grammar Detective" scenarios with his "mistakes."
    -   Can repeat key "Grammar Words" or rules in a memorable way.
    -   Adds an element of fun and surprise.
    -   Represents the "EXPERIENCE" stage in a playful manner.
-   **Visual Cues:** A colourful parrot, often perched on a shoulder or a classroom object. Might have a thought bubble with a grammatically incorrect sentence.

## 4. CHARACTER VOICE AND DIALOGUE GUIDELINES

-   **Age-Appropriate Language:** Dialogue should sound natural for 10-year-olds, avoiding overly complex sentence structures or slang that might quickly date the material.
-   **Distinct Voices:** Maya's dialogue is more reflective, Rohan's is more active, and Ms. Sharma's is guiding. Leo's dialogue is often short, sometimes grammatically flawed, and humorous.
-   **Purposeful Dialogue:** Every piece of dialogue should contribute to the learning objective, either by introducing a concept, demonstrating usage, or setting up an activity.
-   **Cultural Sensitivity:** Names and interactions should be culturally appropriate and inclusive.

---

## 5. VISUAL REPRESENTATION GUIDELINES

-   **Consistency:** Character designs must be consistent across all illustrations.
-   **Expressiveness:** Characters should convey emotions and actions clearly to support the narrative and pedagogical intent.
-   **Diversity:** Represent a diverse classroom environment.
-   **Simplicity:** Designs should be clear and uncluttered, suitable for educational materials.
-   **Dynamic Poses:** Characters should be shown in various engaging poses, interacting with each other and their environment.
