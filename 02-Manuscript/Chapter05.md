The learner can:
- Define a pronoun and explain its purpose.
- Identify personal pronouns in sentences.
- Differentiate between subject and object pronouns.
- Use the correct pronoun for a given noun (person and number).

## 5. CONCEPT DISCOVERY (WHAT IS A PRONOUN?)

A **pronoun** is a word that is used in place of a noun.

Using pronouns helps us avoid repeating the same nouns over and over again.

-   **Without Pronoun:** **Maya** has a new book. **Maya** is reading the book.
-   **With Pronoun:** **Maya** has a new book. **She** is reading **it**.

Here, `She` replaces `Maya`, and `it` replaces `the book`.

---

## 6. CONCEPT EXPLANATION (PERSONAL PRONOUNS)

The most common pronouns are **personal pronouns**. They change their form depending on their job in a sentence. They can be a **subject** (the one doing the action) or an **object** (the one receiving the action).

### Subject Pronouns

**Subject pronouns** perform the action in a sentence. They usually come before the verb.

*Example:* **He** threw the ball. (`He` is the one doing the throwing.)

### Object Pronouns

**Object pronouns** receive the action in a sentence. They usually come after the verb.

*Example:* Rohan threw the ball to **him**. (`him` is the one receiving the ball.)

### The Pronoun Team

Let's meet the whole team of personal pronouns!

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **First Person**<br>(the person speaking) | **I** (Subject)<br>**me** (Object) | **we** (Subject)<br>**us** (Object) |
| **Second Person**<br>(the person spoken to) | **you** (Subject)<br>**you** (Object) | **you** (Subject)<br>**you** (Object) |
| **Third Person**<br>(the person/thing spoken about) | **he, she, it** (Subject)<br>**him, her, it** (Object) | **they** (Subject)<br>**them** (Object) |

## 7. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Ms. Sharma:** "Let's practice replacing nouns with the correct pronouns."

**Original Sentence:** `Rohan loves cricket. Rohan plays cricket every day.`

**Let's Think Step-by-Step:**

1.  **Identify the noun being repeated:** `Rohan`.
2.  **What pronoun can replace `Rohan`?** `Rohan` is a single boy being spoken about (Third Person, Singular, Masculine). The pronoun is `he`.
3.  **Is `Rohan` the subject or object in the second sentence?** He is the one playing, so he is the subject. We need the subject pronoun.
4.  **Replace the noun:** `He`.

**Revised Sentence:** `Rohan loves cricket. **He** plays cricket every day.`

**Another Example:**

**Original Sentence:** `Ms. Sharma gave the books to Maya and Rohan.`

1.  **Identify the nouns to be replaced:** `Maya and Rohan`.
2.  **What pronoun can replace `Maya and Rohan`?** This is a group of people being spoken about (Third Person, Plural). The pronoun is `they` or `them`.
3.  **Are they the subject or object?** They are receiving the books from Ms. Sharma, so they are the object. We need the object pronoun.
4.  **Replace the nouns:** `them`.

**Revised Sentence:** `Ms. Sharma gave the books to **them**.`

## 10. GRAMMAR DETECTIVE (COMMON MISTAKES)

**Leo:** (Squawks proudly) "**Me and Maya** are best friends!"

**Ms. Sharma:** "That's lovely, Leo! But your sentence has a common mistake. When you talk about yourself and someone else, it's polite to say the other person's name first. Also, we need to choose between 'I' and 'me'."

**The 'Cover-Up' Trick:**
To know if you should use 'I' or 'me', cover up the other person's name and see if the sentence still makes sense.

-   Leo said: "**Me** are best friends." (This sounds wrong.)
-   Try with 'I': "**I** am a best friend." (This sounds correct!)

So, the correct sentence is: "**Maya and I** are best friends."

**Your Turn, Detective!**

Correct the pronoun mistakes in these sentences.

1.  Me and Rohan went to the market.
2.  Her gave me a pencil.
3.  My dad bought a gift for my brother and I.

**Watch Out! `me` vs. `my`**

-   Use **me** when you are the object (receiving the action).
    *   *Example:* Mom gave the apple to **me**.
-   Use **my** to show that something belongs to you. It comes before a noun.
    *   *Example:* That is **my** apple.

## 13. LET'S SUMMARISE

In this chapter, we learned how to use pronouns to make our sentences better.

-   **Pronouns** are words that take the place of nouns.
-   **Subject Pronouns** do the action (`I, you, he, she, it, we, they`).
-   **Object Pronouns** receive the action (`me, you, him, her, it, us, them`).

> **Key Idea:** Using pronouns makes your writing and speaking smooth, clear, and less repetitive!
