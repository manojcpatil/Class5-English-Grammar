## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
This is the final chapter of the book, and its purpose is different from every chapter before it, including Chapter 27 (Grammar Detective) and Chapter 28 (Language Workshop). Those chapters still taught learners *how* to integrate skills, with worked examples and scaffolded practice. This chapter instead offers learners a chance to **show what they know**, independently, in a format close to a real class test — while still including a full answer key and guidance for what to revise next. Per `ChapterTemplate.md` Section 38 ("Do Not Force the Template"), this chapter deliberately omits some scaffolding sections (Language Experience, Look and Notice, Grammar Word, Worked Example) that do not suit a revision-and-testing chapter, and instead expands the practice materials themselves.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Complete a full, mixed-section practice test independently, covering grammar, vocabulary, sentence construction, editing, and writing.
- Use test-taking strategies: reading instructions carefully, allocating time sensibly, and checking answers before finishing.
- Mark their own or a partner's completed test against a full answer key.
- Identify, from their own results, one or two areas that would benefit from further revision, and know which earlier chapter to revisit.

## 5. TEST-TAKING STRATEGY

Before beginning either practice test, keep these strategies in mind:

| Strategy | Why It Helps |
|---|---|
| Read every instruction fully before answering | Some sections ask for more than one thing (e.g., "correct the sentence *and* explain why") |
| Attempt every section, even briefly | A partial answer usually earns more than a blank one |
| Watch the time allotted for each section | Spending too long on one section can leave too little time for others |
| Leave a few minutes at the end to check your work | Many small errors (a missing full stop, a wrong verb ending) are easy to catch on a second look |
| If a question feels unfamiliar, move on and return to it later | Getting stuck on one question can cost time you need elsewhere |

## 6. PRACTICE TEST A

**Suggested time: 60 minutes. Total marks: 50.**

### Section 1 — Grammar Identification (10 marks)

For each sentence, identify the part of speech, tense, or grammar feature asked for.

1. Underline the noun and circle the verb: *The children played happily in the garden.* (2 marks)
2. What tense is this sentence? *"Maya will visit her cousin next week."* (1 mark)
3. Identify the adjective and the noun it describes: *The clever fox tricked the crow.* (2 marks)
4. Is the underlined verb an action verb or a verb of being? *Leo **is** a clever parrot.* (1 mark)
5. Identify the conjunction and explain what relationship it shows: *"I wanted to play outside, but it started raining."* (2 marks)
6. Choose the correct article: *I saw ___ (a/an) elephant at the zoo.* (1 mark)
7. Is this sentence active or passive? *"The cake was baked by Maya."* (1 mark)

### Section 2 — Vocabulary (10 marks)

1. Give one synonym and one antonym for "happy." (2 marks)
2. Fill in the correct homophone: *___ (Their/There/They're) going to the market.* (1 mark)
3. Fill in the correct homophone: *___ (Its/It's) raining today.* (1 mark)
4. Add the prefix "un-" to "kind" and explain the new word's meaning. (2 marks)
5. Add the suffix "-ful" to "care" and use the new word in a sentence of your own. (2 marks)
6. Rewrite this sentence, replacing the vague word with a more precise one: *"The food was nice."* (2 marks)

### Section 3 — Subject-Verb Agreement and Tense (10 marks)

1. Choose the correct verb: *The basket of apples (is/are) heavy.* (1 mark)
2. Correct this sentence: *"The children plays outside every afternoon."* (2 marks)
3. Correct this sentence: *"Yesterday, we go to the fair and we eated ice cream."* (3 marks)
4. Complete with the correct present continuous form: *Look! The dog ___ (bark) at the gate.* (1 mark)
5. Rewrite this sentence in the future tense: *"Leo eats seeds."* (1 mark)
6. Correct the tense-consistency error: *"Every day, Maya feeds her fish. Yesterday, she cleans the tank."* (2 marks)

### Section 4 — Editing (10 marks)

Read the passage below and correct all the errors you can find (agreement, tense, punctuation, word choice, and sentence construction). There are eight errors in total.

> "yesterday my family go to the beach. we sees lots of shells and my brother collect some. its a very hot day and everyone were tired by the evening. i loves the beach."

### Section 5 — Writing (10 marks)

Write a short paragraph (5-7 sentences) describing a place that is special to you. Include:
- a clear topic sentence,
- at least two sensory details,
- consistent tense throughout.

## 7. PRACTICE TEST B

**Suggested time: 60 minutes. Total marks: 50.**

### Section 1 — Grammar Identification (10 marks)

1. Underline the subject and circle the predicate: *The old clock struck thirteen.* (2 marks)
2. Identify the preposition and its kind (place, time, or direction): *"The meeting will start at 10 o'clock."* (2 marks)
3. Identify the pronoun and say whether it is a subject or object pronoun: *"Rohan is waiting for us."* (2 marks)
4. What kind of sentence is this — statement, question, command, or exclamation? *"What a wonderful surprise!"* (1 mark)
5. Identify the adverb and the word it describes: *"She sang beautifully."* (2 marks)
6. Change this active sentence into the passive voice: *"The teacher checked our homework."* (1 mark)

### Section 2 — Vocabulary (10 marks)

1. Sort these words into three pairs, and say whether each pair is a synonym pair or an antonym pair: *happy, sad, joyful, tiny, huge, cheerful.* (3 marks)
2. Fill in the correct homophone: *I have ___ (to/too/two) pencils.* (1 mark)
3. What one word means "a person who cannot hear"? (1 mark)
4. Explain the difference in meaning between "careful" and "careless." (2 marks)
5. Rewrite this sentence to remove the repeated word: *"The tall boy climbed the tall tree to see the tall building."* (3 marks)

### Section 3 — Sentence Construction and Transformation (10 marks)

1. Combine these two sentences using a suitable joining word: *"It was raining. We stayed inside."* (2 marks)
2. Change this sentence from direct to reported speech: *Maya said, "I am tired."* (2 marks)
3. Change this sentence from reported to direct speech, adding correct punctuation: *Rohan said that he was hungry.* (2 marks)
4. Expand this simple sentence with two details (when and where): *"The children laughed."* (2 marks)
5. Fix the word order: *"Quickly the girl the ball threw."* (2 marks)

### Section 4 — Editing (10 marks)

Read the passage below and correct all the errors you can find (agreement, tense, punctuation, word choice, and sentence construction). There are nine errors in total.

> "my sister and me is going to the library. we reads books there every saturday. last week she borrow three books and I borrows two. its always quiet inside and everyone whisper. I loves the library."

### Section 5 — Writing (10 marks)

Choose **one**:
- Write a short informal letter (6-8 lines) to a friend, telling them about something exciting that happened recently. Include all five parts of an informal letter.
- Write a short story completion (5-7 sentences) continuing this opener: *"The old clock in the hallway struck thirteen, and that was when everything changed."* Keep your tense consistent throughout, and include at least one line of correctly punctuated dialogue.

## 14. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students often treat a low score on a practice test as a final judgement rather than as diagnostic information, which can discourage further revision exactly when it would help most.

Support strategy: Before returning marked practice tests, remind the class explicitly that the purpose of Section 9 ("Where to Revise") is to turn every wrong answer into a specific, findable next step — not a final grade. Consider having students set one personal revision goal (e.g., "revise Chapter 14 before Friday") rather than only recording a score.

Extension: Once both practice tests have been completed and marked, consider setting a third, teacher-authored practice test that mixes items from Tests A and B in a new order, to check whether performance holds up outside the exact format already seen.

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to write one full extra section (any of the five types used here) of their own, complete with a working answer key, for the rest of the class to attempt.
