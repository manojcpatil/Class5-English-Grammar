## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
Having explored synonyms, antonyms, and homophones, learners now learn how new words are *built* from smaller parts. Understanding prefixes, suffixes, and word families helps learners guess the meaning of unfamiliar words and expand their vocabulary efficiently. One-word substitutions also make writing more concise and precise.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Identify a root word inside a longer word.
- Add simple prefixes and suffixes to change a word's meaning.
- Recognise words belonging to the same word family.
- Use a one-word expression instead of a longer phrase where appropriate.

## 7. DISCOVER / EXPLICIT TEACHING

### Explicit explanation

Many English words are built from a **root word** plus a **prefix** (added to the front) or a **suffix** (added to the end).

| Part | Position | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root word | — | Carries the main meaning | kind, care, play |
| Prefix | Front | Changes meaning, often to opposite | un + happy = unhappy |
| Suffix | End | Changes word form or adds meaning | kind + ness = kindness |

---

## 8. GRAMMAR WORD

## Grammar Word: PREFIX

A **prefix** is a word part added to the **beginning** of a root word to change its meaning.

### Examples

- un + happy = unhappy (not happy)
- re + write = rewrite (write again)
- dis + agree = disagree (not agree)

### Non-examples / Contrast

- "-ness" added to "kind" (kindness) is not a prefix — it is added to the end, so it is a suffix.

### Quick Check

Add "un-" to "kind." What is the new word and its meaning? *(unkind — not kind)*

## Grammar Word: SUFFIX

A **suffix** is a word part added to the **end** of a root word to change its form or meaning.

### Examples

- care + ful = careful (full of care)
- care + less = careless (without care)
- teach + er = teacher (a person who teaches)

### Non-examples / Contrast

- "re-" added to "write" (rewrite) is not a suffix — it is added to the beginning, so it is a prefix.

### Quick Check

Add "-less" to "hope." What is the new word and its meaning? *(hopeless — without hope)*

---

## 9. CORE EXPLANATION

**Prefixes often reverse or change meaning**
→ Example: agree → **dis**agree (opposite)
→ Contrast: appear → **dis**appear (opposite: to stop being visible)
→ Check: Does the prefix flip the root word's meaning to its opposite, or add "again" (re-)?

**Suffixes often change word form**
→ Example: help (verb) → help**ful** (adjective, "full of help")
→ Contrast: help (verb) → help**er** (noun, "a person who helps")
→ Check: Does the suffix turn the word into a describing word (adjective) or a naming word (noun)?

**Word families share a root**
→ Example: play, player, playful, playground — all share the root "play."
→ Contrast: "plate" looks similar but is not part of the "play" word family — it has a different root meaning entirely.
→ Check: Does the word truly share the meaning of the root, not just similar letters?

**One-word expressions**
→ Example: "a person who cannot hear" → **deaf**; "a place where books are kept" → **library**
→ Contrast: Using the full phrase is not wrong, but the single word is often more precise and efficient.
→ Check: Is there a single word that captures the same meaning as the phrase?

---

## 10. TYPES / CLASSIFICATIONS

### Common Prefixes

Name: un-, re-, dis-
Meaning: un- (not), re- (again), dis- (not/opposite)
Example: unkind, rewrite, disappear
Contrast: Not every prefix means the same thing — always check the meaning it gives to the specific root word.
Quick Check: What does "re-" mean in "replay"? *(again — to play again)*

### Common Suffixes

Name: -ful, -less, -ly, -er
Meaning: -ful (full of), -less (without), -ly (in a certain way), -er (a person who does something)
Example: careful, careless, quickly, teacher
Contrast: "-ful" and "-less" often form opposite pairs from the same root (careful/careless), while "-ly" usually changes an adjective into an adverb (quick → quickly).
Quick Check: What does "-less" mean in "fearless"? *(without fear)*

### One-Word Expressions

Name: Useful one-word substitutions
Meaning: A single word that replaces a common longer phrase.
Example: "a person who cannot hear" = deaf; "a place where planes take off and land" = airport; "a person who studies" = student
Contrast: These are not synonyms of a single word, but neat replacements for a whole descriptive phrase.
Quick Check: What one word means "a place where books are kept"? *(library)*

## 11. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Task:** Build a new word by adding the correct prefix or suffix, and explain the meaning: "agree" + "not agreeing."

Step 1: Identify the root word — "agree."

Step 2: Identify the meaning needed — "not agreeing" suggests an opposite meaning.

Step 3: Choose the correct prefix — "dis-" means "not," so "dis" + "agree" = "disagree."

Answer: **disagree** — meaning "to not agree."

Why: The prefix "dis-" reverses the meaning of the root word "agree," creating a new word with the opposite meaning.

## 12. COMPARE AND NOTICE

**Before:** "The boy was full of care when he carried the eggs." (long phrase)
**After:** "The boy was **careful** when he carried the eggs." (one-word expression)

What changed? The phrase "full of care" was replaced by the single suffixed word "careful."
Why? The single word expresses the same idea more concisely and naturally.

## 16. COMMON CONFUSION

### WATCH OUT!

Common confusion: Learners sometimes combine two word-building parts that do not belong together (like "unkindful") or use a noun form ("carelessness") where an adjective ("careless") is needed.

How to check: Ask, "Does this word part actually exist for this root, and does the resulting word fit the sentence as a describing word, naming word, or action word?"

Correct examples: unkind (not unkindful), careless (adjective) vs. carelessness (noun — "His carelessness caused the accident").

## 25. SUMMARY

- Words are often built from a **root word** plus a **prefix** (front) or **suffix** (end).
- Prefixes like **un-**, **re-**, and **dis-** change meaning, often to the opposite.
- Suffixes like **-ful**, **-less**, **-ly**, and **-er** change a word's form or add meaning.
- Words sharing a root form a **word family**.
- A **one-word expression** can often replace a longer phrase.

## 26. KEY TERMS

| Term | Simple Meaning |
|---|---|
| Root word | The main word that carries the core meaning |

| Suffix | A word part added to the end to change form or meaning |
| Word family | A group of words sharing the same root |
| One-word expression | A single word that replaces a longer phrase |

## 30. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students may over-apply a prefix or suffix to a root word where it does not naturally combine (e.g., "unkindful"), or confuse noun and adjective suffix forms.

Support strategy: Build a class word-family wall, adding new root words and their prefixed/suffixed forms as they are learned, checking each combination is a real word.

Extension: Introduce a few more prefixes and suffixes gradually (pre-, mis-, -able) once the core set is secure.

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to build the longest correct word chain they can from a single root word (e.g., care → careful → carefully).
