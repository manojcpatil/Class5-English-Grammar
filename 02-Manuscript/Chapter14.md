## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
Having mastered subject-verb agreement, learners are ready to explore how verbs also change to show *when* something happens. Tense is one of the most important tools for clear communication, and errors in tense are among the most common mistakes young writers make. This chapter builds a solid foundation in the three main time zones of English before later chapters refine how tenses are used in connected writing.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Recognise present, past, and future tense verbs.
- Form regular past tense verbs correctly (-ed).
- Recall common irregular past tense verbs.
- Choose the correct tense based on time clues in a sentence.
- Use time expressions correctly with each tense.

## 7. DISCOVER / EXPLICIT TEACHING

### Explicit explanation

Verbs change their form to show **when** an action happens. This is called **tense**. There are three main time zones:

| Time Zone | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Present | Happening now, or always true, or a habit | Maya **plays** the piano. |
| Past | Already happened | Maya **played** the piano yesterday. |
| Future | Will happen later | Maya **will play** the piano tomorrow. |

---

## 8. GRAMMAR WORD

## Grammar Word: TENSE

**Tense** is the form of a verb that shows the time of an action — present, past, or future.

### Examples

- Present: Rohan **runs** fast.
- Past: Rohan **ran** fast yesterday.
- Future: Rohan **will run** fast tomorrow.

### Non-examples / Contrast

- "Rohan fast run" — no clear tense, not a real sentence.
- Mixing tenses in one sentence without reason: "Rohan runs and jumped." (confusing, usually incorrect)

### Quick Check

Which time zone: "The sun **rises** in the east."?
*(Answer: Present — an always-true fact.)*

---

## 9. CORE EXPLANATION

**Present tense** tells us about now, habits, or facts that are always true.
→ Example: Birds **fly** south in winter.
→ Contrast with past: Birds **flew** south last winter.
→ Example: The class **starts** at nine o'clock.
→ Check: Is this happening regularly? Then it is present tense.

**Past tense** tells us about something that has already finished.
→ Example: We **visited** the zoo last week.
→ Contrast with future: We **will visit** the zoo next week.
→ Example: She **wrote** a letter yesterday.
→ Check: Look for a past time word like *yesterday*, *last week*, *ago*.

**Future tense** tells us about something that has not happened yet.
→ Example: We **will visit** the museum next month.
→ Contrast with present: We **visit** the museum every month.
→ Check: Look for *will*, *shall*, or a future time word like *tomorrow*, *next year*.

---

## 10. TYPES / CLASSIFICATIONS

### Regular Verbs (Past Tense)

Name: Regular past tense
Meaning: Formed by adding **-ed** to the base verb.
Example: walk → walk**ed**, play → play**ed**, want → want**ed**
Contrast: Some verbs double the final consonant: stop → stop**ped**; some drop -e and add -ed: like → lik**ed**.
Quick Check: What is the past tense of "jump"? *(jumped)*

### Irregular Verbs (Past Tense)

Name: Irregular past tense
Meaning: These verbs do **not** follow the -ed rule; they change form completely.
Example: go → **went**, eat → **ate**, see → **saw**, is/are → **was/were**, have → **had**, do → **did**
Contrast: There is no single rule — these must be learned and remembered.
Quick Check: What is the past tense of "go"? *(went, not "goed")*

### Common Irregular Verbs Table

| Present | Past |
|---|---|
| go | went |
| eat | ate |
| see | saw |
| have | had |
| do | did |
| write | wrote |
| run | ran |
| come | came |
| is/am/are | was/were |
| take | took |

## 11. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Sentence:** "Yesterday, Maya ___ (go) to the library and ___ (read) two books."

Step 1: Look for a time clue. "Yesterday" tells us this is the past.

Step 2: Check each verb. "Go" is irregular in the past — it becomes "went," not "goed."

Step 3: Check the second verb. "Read" is regular in spelling but pronounced differently; its past form is also "read" (spelled the same, said differently).

Answer: "Yesterday, Maya **went** to the library and **read** two books."

Why: The time word "yesterday" signals past tense, so both verbs must match that time zone.

---

## 12. COMPARE AND NOTICE

**Incorrect:** Tomorrow, Rohan **played** cricket.
**Correct:** Tomorrow, Rohan **will play** cricket.

What changed? The verb changed from past ("played") to future ("will play").
Why? The time word "tomorrow" tells us the action has not happened yet.

## 16. COMMON CONFUSION

### WATCH OUT!

Common confusion: Learners often add **-ed** to irregular verbs, producing forms like "goed," "eated," or "runned."

How to check: Ask, "Is this one of the special verbs that changes completely?" If unsure, check the irregular verbs table.

Correct examples: went (not goed), ate (not eated), ran (not runned).

## 25. SUMMARY

- Tense shows **when** an action happens: present, past, or future.
- Regular verbs add **-ed** for the past tense.
- Irregular verbs change completely in the past tense and must be memorised.
- Time words like *yesterday*, *now*, and *tomorrow* are strong clues for choosing the correct tense.

## 30. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students often regularise irregular verbs ("goed," "eated," "runned") because the -ed rule is the first one they learn well.

Support strategy: Keep a visible classroom chart of the ten most common irregular verbs; drill orally before written practice.

Extension: Introduce a few additional irregular verbs (bring/brought, buy/bought, catch/caught).

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to sort irregular verbs by pattern (e.g., vowel change only: run/ran; complete change: go/went).
