## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
Learners already know how to punctuate direct speech (Chapter 16) and how pronouns and tenses work (Chapters 5 and 14). This chapter brings those skills together for a genuinely difficult task: retelling what someone said, rather than quoting their exact words. Full reported speech (with every possible tense backshift) is a demanding skill even for older learners, so this chapter is deliberately narrow — it introduces the idea and the single most common transformation pattern, rather than attempting complete mastery.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Explain the difference between direct speech and reported speech.
- Identify direct speech (using quotation marks) in a passage.
- Change a simple statement from direct speech to reported speech, adjusting the pronoun and the tense.
- Change a simple reported statement back into direct speech.

## 7. DISCOVER / EXPLICIT TEACHING

### Explicit explanation

**Direct speech** shows a speaker's exact words, inside quotation marks.
**Reported speech** tells someone else what was said, without quotation marks, usually with small changes to pronouns and tense.

| Feature | Direct Speech | Reported Speech |
|---|---|---|
| Quotation marks | Yes | No |
| Pronoun | Speaker's own (I, my) | Changed to match the reporter's view (he, she, his, her) |
| Verb tense | Speaker's original tense | Usually shifts one step into the past |
| Example | Rohan said, "**I am** happy." | Rohan said that **he was** happy. |

---

## 8. GRAMMAR WORD

## Grammar Word: DIRECT SPEECH AND REPORTED SPEECH

**Direct speech** is a speaker's exact words, shown inside quotation marks.
**Reported speech** is a retelling of what someone said, without their exact words or quotation marks.

### Examples

- Direct: Maya said, "I am reading a book."
- Reported: Maya said that she was reading a book.

### Non-examples / Contrast

- "Maya said 'I am reading a book'" without a comma or capital letter is direct speech with incorrect punctuation, not reported speech — reported speech has no quotation marks at all.

### Quick Check

Is this direct or reported speech? "Rohan said that he was late." *(Reported speech — no quotation marks, and the pronoun/tense have changed.)*

---

## 9. CORE EXPLANATION

**Changing the pronoun**
→ Example: Rohan said, "**I** am ready." → Rohan said that **he** was ready.
→ Contrast: Maya said, "**I** am ready." → Maya said that **she** was ready.
→ Check: Whose exact words were "I"? Replace "I" with the pronoun that refers to that same speaker (he/she).

**Shifting the tense (present → past)**
→ Example: Rohan said, "I **am** hungry." → Rohan said that he **was** hungry.
→ Example: Maya said, "I **like** stories." → Maya said that she **liked** stories.
→ Contrast: This chapter only covers the shift from simple present (am/is/are, like/likes) to simple past (was/were, liked) — other tense shifts are not covered here.
→ Check: Was the reported sentence originally in the simple present? Then shift it one step into the simple past.

**Using "that" to introduce reported speech**
→ Example: Rohan said **that** he was tired.
→ Contrast: "That" can sometimes be left out in informal speech ("Rohan said he was tired"), but including it makes the sentence structure clearer for learners at this stage.
→ Check: Does the reported sentence flow smoothly with "that" joining the reporting verb to the reported idea?

---

## 10. TYPES / CLASSIFICATIONS

### Reporting Verbs

Name: Reporting verbs
Meaning: The verb used to introduce reported speech, most commonly "said" or "told."
Example: Rohan **said** that he was hungry. / Rohan **told** Maya that he was hungry.
Contrast: "Said" does not need a listener named ("Rohan said that..."), while "told" needs a listener named ("Rohan told Maya that...").
Quick Check: Which reporting verb needs a listener's name: "said" or "told"? *(told)*

### Pronoun Shift Pairs

Name: Common pronoun shifts
Meaning: The most common pronoun changes when moving from direct to reported speech.
Example: I → he/she; my → his/her; we → they
Contrast: Not every pronoun changes — if you are reporting your own earlier words, "I" may stay "I" (e.g., "I said that I was tired").
Quick Check: If Maya says, "I am ready," and you report her words, what pronoun replaces "I"? *(she)*

---

## 11. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Sentence to report:** Rohan said, "I am the fastest runner in the class."

Step 1: Remove the quotation marks and add "that" to join the reporting verb to the idea.

Step 2: Change the pronoun. "I" refers to Rohan, so it becomes "he."

Step 3: Change the tense. "am" (simple present) becomes "was" (simple past).

Answer: **Rohan said that he was the fastest runner in the class.**

Why: The exact words are no longer quoted, the pronoun now correctly refers to Rohan from an outside speaker's point of view, and the tense has shifted one step into the past, as is usual when reporting.

---

## 12. COMPARE AND NOTICE

**Direct:** Maya said, "I **love** my new bicycle."
**Reported:** Maya said that she **loved** her new bicycle.

What changed? "I" became "she," "my" became "her," "love" became "loved," and the quotation marks were removed.
Why? Reported speech retells the idea from someone else's point of view, using the past tense to show that the words were spoken earlier.

## 16. COMMON CONFUSION

### WATCH OUT!

Common confusion: Learners often forget to change the pronoun, the tense, or both when moving from direct to reported speech, producing sentences like "Rohan said that I am hungry" (should be "he was hungry") when reporting someone else's words.

How to check: Ask, "Whose words am I reporting, and from whose point of view am I now speaking? Does my pronoun match the person being reported, and has my tense shifted into the past?"

Correct examples: Rohan said, "I am happy." → Rohan said that **he was** happy (not "I am happy" or "he is happy").

## 25. SUMMARY

- **Direct speech** shows a speaker's exact words inside quotation marks.
- **Reported speech** retells what was said, without quotation marks.
- Pronouns usually change to match the reporter's point of view (I → he/she, my → his/her).
- The tense usually shifts one step into the past (am/is/are → was/were; like/likes → liked).
- Common reporting verbs include "said" and "told."

## 30. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students often change either the pronoun or the tense but not both, or attempt to apply reported speech to questions and commands before they are ready, since this chapter deliberately limits scope to statements only.

Editorial note: All examples and exercises in this chapter must use only the simple present (am/is/are, like/likes) to simple past (was/were, liked) shift. Do not introduce continuous forms ("is going" → "was going") or modal backshifts ("will" → "would") anywhere in this chapter, including in "Language in Real Life" or practice items — these are out of scope and were removed during review (see Log.md DEC-034).

Support strategy: Practise the pronoun change and the tense change as two separate, explicit steps before combining them, using the same sentence each time so learners can isolate what changes.

Extension: Briefly mention that reporting questions and commands ("she asked if...", "he told me to...") uses different patterns, to be covered in a later grade.

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to report a short exchange between two speakers, correctly tracking two different pronoun shifts in the same passage.
