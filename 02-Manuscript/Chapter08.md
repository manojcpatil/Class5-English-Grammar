## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
After nouns, pronouns, and adjectives, verbs are the next crucial building block of sentences. Understanding verbs allows students to grasp the core of what is happening in a sentence, forming the basis for later concepts like tense, subject-verb agreement, and sentence structure.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Define a verb as a word that shows action or state of being.
- Identify verbs in sentences.
- Distinguish between action verbs and verbs of being.
- Understand that a verb is essential for a complete sentence.

## 5. CONCEPT DISCOVERY (WHAT IS A VERB?)

A **verb** is a word that shows an **action** or a **state of being**.

Every complete sentence must have a verb. It tells us what the noun or pronoun is doing, or what it is like.

### A. Action Verbs

**Action verbs** tell us what the subject of the sentence is doing.

*Examples:*
-   The bird **sings** beautifully.
-   Rohan **plays** football.
-   Maya **reads** a book.

### B. Verbs of Being (State of Being Verbs)

**Verbs of being** do not show action. They tell us what the subject *is* or *is like*. They connect the subject to a description or another noun.

*Examples:*
-   Leo **is** a parrot.
-   Ms. Sharma **is** kind.
-   They **are** happy.

---

## 6. IDENTIFYING VERBS

To find the verb in a sentence, ask yourself:

1.  **What is the subject doing?** (For action verbs)
2.  **What is the subject?** or **What is the subject like?** (For verbs of being)

*Example 1:* The **dog** `barked` loudly.
-   What did the dog *do*? It `barked`. (`barked` is an action verb.)

*Example 2:* The **flowers** `are` beautiful.
-   What are the flowers *like*? They `are` beautiful. (`are` is a verb of being.)

---

## 7. THE SUBJECT-VERB RELATIONSHIP

Every complete sentence needs a **subject** and a **verb**.

-   The **subject** is the noun or pronoun that performs the action or is in a certain state.
-   The **verb** tells us what the subject does or is.

*Example:* **Rohan** `runs` fast.
-   **Subject:** Rohan (who runs?)
-   **Verb:** runs (what does Rohan do?)

*Example:* **Maya** `is` happy.
-   **Subject:** Maya (who is happy?)
-   **Verb:** is (what is Maya like?)

## 12. LET'S SUMMARISE

In this chapter, we learned that a **verb** is the most important word in a sentence.

-   A verb shows **action** or a **state of being**.

| Verb Type | What it Does | Example |

## 20. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students sometimes think only "doing words" count as verbs and overlook verbs of being (is, am, are), especially when no obvious action is happening.

Support strategy: Use the "what is the subject doing, or what is the subject like?" two-question check from Section 6 consistently across activities.

Extension: Briefly preview helping verbs (is/was + -ing) as a bridge toward tense chapters later in the book.

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to rewrite a paragraph replacing every plain verb ("went," "said") with a more vivid, specific verb.
