## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
Nouns are the fundamental building blocks of sentences. Understanding what a noun is allows students to identify the subject of a sentence and build a framework for understanding other parts of speech, like pronouns and adjectives, which relate to nouns.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Define a noun as a word that names a person, place, animal, or thing.
- Identify nouns in sentences.
- Differentiate between common and proper nouns.
- Apply capitalization rules for proper nouns.

## 5. CONCEPT DISCOVERY (WHAT IS A NOUN?)

A **noun** is a word that names a **person**, a **place**, an **animal**, or a **thing**.

Nouns are also called **naming words**. Everything around us has a name.

| **Person** | **Place** | **Animal** | **Thing** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| boy | school | lion | table |
| doctor | city | parrot | computer |
| Maya | park | fish | pencil |
| teacher | India | dog | happiness |

Think about the words from the game:
- **Rohan**, **Maya**, **Leo** are names of a person and an animal.
- The **classroom** is a place.
- The **desk**, **whiteboard**, **window**, **trees**, and **book** are all things.

All these naming words are **nouns**.

## 6. CONCEPT EXPLANATION (COMMON AND PROPER NOUNS)

**Ms. Sharma:** "In our game, Rohan, you mentioned 'desk', and Maya, you mentioned 'Rohan'. Both are nouns, but are they the same type of noun?"

**Maya:** "I think they are different. 'Desk' can mean any desk, but 'Rohan' is the special name for one person."

**Ms. Sharma:** "Perfectly explained, Maya! You've discovered the difference between **Common Nouns** and **Proper Nouns**."

### Common Nouns

A **common noun** is the general, ordinary name of a person, place, animal, or thing.

Examples:
- The **boy** kicked the **ball**.
- We live in a **city**.
- My **dog** loves to play in the **park**.

Common nouns do not begin with a capital letter unless they start a sentence.

### Proper Nouns

A **proper noun** is the special name of a particular person, place, animal, or thing.

Examples:
- **Rohan** kicked the ball.
- We live in **Mumbai**.
- My dog, **Bruno**, loves to play in **City Park**.

> **Remember!**
> Proper nouns **always** begin with a capital letter.

Let's compare them:

| Common Noun | Proper Noun |
| :--- | :--- |
| girl | Maya, Anya |
| country | India, Japan |
| river | Ganga, Nile |
| day | Monday, Sunday |
| month | January, July |
| festival | Diwali, Christmas |
| building | Taj Mahal |

## 7. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Ms. Sharma:** "Let's look at a sentence and find the common and proper nouns."

**Sentence:** `Rohan and his sister went to Delhi during the summer holidays.`

**Let's Think Step-by-Step:**

1.  **Find all the nouns (naming words):** `Rohan`, `sister`, `Delhi`, `summer`, `holidays`.
2.  **Ask: Is it a general name or a special name?**
    -   `Rohan`: Special name for a person. → **Proper Noun**
    -   `sister`: General name for a person. → **Common Noun**
    -   `Delhi`: Special name for a place (a city). → **Proper Noun**
    -   `summer`: General name for a season (thing). → **Common Noun**
    -   `holidays`: General name for a period of time (thing). → **Common Noun**

## 8. CONCEPT EXPLANATION (SINGULAR AND PLURAL NOUNS)

**Ms. Sharma:** "Excellent work! Now, let's look at nouns in another way. Rohan, you said you saw a 'desk', not 'desks'. That tells us about the number."

**Rohan:** "You mean one book or many books?"

**Ms. Sharma:** "Exactly, Rohan! That's the difference between singular and plural nouns."

### Singular Nouns
A **singular noun** names **one** person, place, animal, or thing.
*Examples: cat, flower, city, child*

### Plural Nouns
A **plural noun** names **more than one** person, place, animal, or thing.
*Examples: cats, flowers, cities, children*

### How to Form Plural Nouns

1.  **Add -s:** Most nouns become plural by adding **-s**. (e.g., `book` → `books`)
2.  **Add -es:** Nouns ending in **-s, -ss, -sh, -ch, -x,** or **-z** form their plural by adding **-es**. (e.g., `watch` → `watches`, `box` → `boxes`)
3.  **Change -y to -ies:** Nouns ending in a **consonant + y** change **y** to **i** and add **-es**. (e.g., `baby` → `babies`)

<br>

| Rule | Singular | Plural |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Add **-s** | pen | pens |
| Add **-es** | bus | buses |
| Add **-es** | brush | brushes |
| Add **-es** | class | classes |
| Change **-y** to **-ies** | city | cities |
| Change **-y** to **-ies** | story | stories |

## 11. COMMON CONFUSIONS

Sometimes, words that sound similar can be confusing, or we might forget to capitalize proper nouns.

**Remember:**
-   A **common noun** is a general name. It only starts with a capital letter if it's the first word of a sentence.
    *   Example: The **boy** went to the **city**.
-   A **proper noun** is a special name. It **always** starts with a capital letter.
    *   Example: **Rohan** went to **Mumbai**.

**Watch out for:**
-   Days of the week (**Monday, Tuesday**) are proper nouns.
-   Months of the year (**January, February**) are proper nouns.
-   Names of festivals (**Diwali, Christmas**) are proper nouns.
-   Names of specific places (**India, Mount Everest, Ganga River**) are proper nouns.

## 17. LET'S SUMMARISE

In this chapter, we learned:

-   A **noun** is a naming word for a person, place, animal, or thing.
-   A **common noun** is a general name (e.g., `boy`, `school`).
-   A **proper noun** is a special name and always starts with a capital letter (e.g., `Rohan`, `India`).
-   A **singular noun** means one (`book`).
-   A **plural noun** means more than one (`books`).
-   We form plurals in different ways, like adding **-s**, **-es**, or changing **-y** to **-ies**.

## 26. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students often forget to capitalise proper nouns embedded mid-sentence (days, months, festivals, specific place names), or over-capitalise common nouns by habit.

Support strategy: Keep a running class list of proper nouns spotted during the week (names, places, festivals) as a visible reference chart.

Extension: Introduce nouns that are proper only in certain contexts (e.g., "President," "the North") as a brief enrichment note for advanced learners.

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to find five proper nouns in a piece of their own recent writing and check each is capitalised correctly.
