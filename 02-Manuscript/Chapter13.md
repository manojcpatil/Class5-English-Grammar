## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
Subject-verb agreement is a fundamental rule for grammatical accuracy in English. After learning about subjects, nouns, pronouns, and verbs individually, students are now ready to understand how these elements must work together harmoniously. This chapter is crucial for developing correct sentence construction and editing skills, and sits at the start of Unit 5, which focuses on accuracy and time.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Identify the subject and verb in a sentence.
- Determine if a subject is singular or plural.
- Choose the correct verb form (singular or plural) to match its subject.
- Recognise a few tricky cases, such as words that sit between the subject and the verb.
- Correct errors in subject-verb agreement.

## 7. DISCOVER / EXPLICIT TEACHING

### Explicit explanation

**Subject-Verb Agreement** means that the verb in a sentence must match its subject in number.

- If the subject is **singular** (one), the verb must be **singular**.
- If the subject is **plural** (more than one), the verb must be **plural**.

*Example:*
-   The **cat** **sleeps** all day. (Singular subject "cat" with singular verb "sleeps")
-   The **cats** **sleep** all day. (Plural subject "cats" with plural verb "sleep")

---

## 8. GRAMMAR WORD

## Grammar Word: SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT

**Subject-verb agreement** is the rule that a verb must match its subject in number — a singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.

### Examples

- The **bird sings**. (singular subject, singular verb)
- The **birds sing**. (plural subject, plural verb)

### Non-examples / Contrast

- "The bird sing." (singular subject with a plural-style verb — does not agree)
- "The birds sings." (plural subject with a singular-style verb — does not agree)

### Quick Check

Does this sentence agree? "The children plays outside." *(No — "children" is plural, so it needs "play," not "plays.")*

---

## 9. CORE EXPLANATION

### A. The Basic Rule

Think of it like this: Singular subjects like to have verbs that end in **-s**. Plural subjects like verbs without the **-s**.

| Subject | Verb Form | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Singular** (one) | Verb + **s** | The **bird** sing**s**. |
| **Plural** (many) | Verb (base form) | The **birds** sing. |

This rule applies to most action verbs in the present tense.

### B. Agreement with 'be', 'have', and 'do'

Some of the most common verbs have special forms for singular and plural subjects.

| Verb | Singular Subject | Plural Subject |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **be** | is, was | are, were |
| **have** | has | have |
| **do** | does | do |

*Examples:*
-   He **is** happy. / They **are** happy.
-   She **has** a new book. / They **have** new books.

### C. Special Subjects: 'I' and 'You'

The pronouns **I** and **you** are special.

-   **I** (which is singular) uses a plural verb form.
    -   I **sing**. (not I sings)
    -   I **have** a pen. (not I has)
-   **You** can be singular or plural, but it always uses a plural verb form.
    -   You **are** my friend.
    -   You **sing** well.

---

## 10. TYPES / CLASSIFICATIONS

### Words Between the Subject and the Verb

Name: Interrupting phrase
Meaning: A group of words (often starting with "of," "with," or "along with") that sits between the subject and the verb but does not change the subject's number.
Example: The **basket** of apples **is** on the table. (The subject is "basket," not "apples," so the verb stays singular.)
Contrast: The **apples** in the basket **are** fresh. (Here "apples" really is the subject, so the verb is plural.)
Quick Check: In "The list of names **is/are** on the board," which subject actually controls the verb — "list" or "names"? *(list, so "is")*

### Collective Nouns as Subjects

Name: Collective noun agreement
Meaning: A collective noun (Chapter 4) names a group but is usually treated as a single, singular unit in Class 5-level writing.
Example: The **team** **is** practising for the match. (The team acts as one unit.)
Contrast: The **players** **are** practising for the match. (Here the plural noun "players" is used instead, so the verb is plural.)
Quick Check: Which verb fits: "Our class **is/are** going on a trip"? *(is — "class" is treated as one group)*

## 11. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Ms. Sharma:** "Let's figure out the correct verb for this sentence."

**Sentence:** `Maya (read / reads) a book every day.`

**Let's Think Step-by-Step:**

1.  **Find the subject:** Who or what is doing the action? **Maya**.
2.  **Is the subject singular or plural?** "Maya" is one person, so the subject is **singular**.
3.  **Choose the correct verb form:** For a singular subject, the present tense verb usually ends in **-s**.
4.  **Select the verb:** Between "read" and "reads," the singular form is **reads**.

**Completed Sentence:** `Maya **reads** a book every day.`

**A Trickier Example**

**Sentence:** `The box of pencils (is / are) on the shelf.`

1.  **Find the true subject:** Is it "box" or "pencils"? Read the sentence again — it is really telling us where the *box* is, so the subject is **box**.
2.  **Is "box" singular or plural?** Singular — there is only one box.
3.  **Choose the correct verb:** A singular subject needs "is."

**Completed Sentence:** `The box of pencils **is** on the shelf.`

---

## 12. COMPARE AND NOTICE

**Incorrect:** The bunch of flowers **are** beautiful.
**Correct:** The bunch of flowers **is** beautiful.

What changed? The verb changed from "are" to "is."
Why? The true subject of the sentence is "bunch" (singular), not "flowers." The phrase "of flowers" sits between the subject and the verb but does not change what the subject actually is.

## 15. GRAMMAR DETECTIVE

**Leo:** (Squawks) "My sentences are perfect! Everyone listen!"

1.  The stars **shines** brightly at night.
2.  Rohan and Maya **is** good friends.
3.  The pile of toys **are** on the floor.

**Ms. Sharma:** "Good try, Leo! But the subjects and verbs are not agreeing."

**Let's investigate:**

-   In sentence 1, the subject is "stars," which is plural. A plural subject needs a plural verb. The verb "shines" is singular.
    -   *Correct:* The stars **shine** brightly at night.

-   In sentence 2, the subject is "Rohan and Maya." When two subjects are joined by "and," they form a plural subject. The verb "is" is singular.
    -   *Correct:* Rohan and Maya **are** good friends.

-   In sentence 3, the true subject is "pile" (singular), not "toys." The phrase "of toys" sits between the subject and the verb.
    -   *Correct:* The pile of toys **is** on the floor.

## 16. COMMON CONFUSION

### WATCH OUT!

Common confusion: Learners often make the verb agree with the **nearest** noun instead of the **true subject**, especially when a phrase like "of the class," "along with his friends," or "of toys" sits between the subject and the verb.

How to check: Cover up the interrupting phrase (the words between the subject and the verb) and read the sentence again. Does it still sound right?

Correct examples:
-   The **basket** (of apples) **is** heavy. → Covering "of apples" gives "The basket is heavy," which sounds right.
-   The **students** (in the class) **are** listening. → Covering "in the class" gives "The students are listening," which sounds right.

## 25. SUMMARY

- The **subject** and **verb** in a sentence must agree in number.
- A **singular subject** (one) needs a **singular verb**.
- A **plural subject** (more than one) needs a **plural verb**.
- "I" and "you" are special — they always use the plural-style verb form.
- A phrase between the subject and the verb (like "of apples") does not change what the true subject is.
- Collective nouns (like "team" or "class") are usually treated as singular at this level.

| Subject Type | Verb Rule (Present Tense) | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Singular** | Add **-s** or **-es** | The dog **barks**. |
| **Plural** | Use the base form | The dogs **bark**. |

**Special Verbs:**

| Subject | be | have |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Singular** | is, was | has |
| **Plural** | are, were | have |
| **I** | am, was | have |
| **You** | are, were | have |

> **Key Idea:** Making subjects and verbs agree is like making sure two friends are working together. It makes your sentences correct and clear!

## 30. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students very commonly make the verb agree with the noun closest to it rather than the true subject, especially in sentences with an interrupting phrase ("The basket of apples are..."). This is one of the most persistent subject-verb agreement errors even in older learners' writing.

Support strategy: Teach the "cover-up" check explicitly and have students practise it aloud — physically cover the interrupting phrase with a finger or a strip of paper before reading the sentence again.

Extension: Introduce a few more collective nouns (audience, family, committee) and discuss how they are usually treated as singular in Indian English at this level.

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to find or write a sentence where "and" joins two subjects that describe a single idea and are therefore treated as singular (e.g., "Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast," where the phrase names one dish) — a genuine exception useful for class discussion rather than formal testing.
