**Ms. Sharma:** "Excellent! You just used different forms of adjectives to compare the animals. You used 'small' to describe one animal, 'smaller' to compare two animals, and 'biggest' to compare three or more. These are called the **degrees of comparison** for adjectives."

## 5. CONCEPT DISCOVERY (DEGREES OF COMPARISON)

Adjectives can change their form to show comparison. There are three degrees of comparison:

### A. Positive Degree

The **Positive Degree** of an adjective describes a single noun without making any comparison.

*Example:* The rabbit is **small**.

### B. Comparative Degree

The **Comparative Degree** of an adjective is used to compare two nouns. We usually add **-er** to the adjective or use the word **more** before it.

*Example:* The dog is **smaller** than the elephant.

### C. Superlative Degree

The **Superlative Degree** of an adjective is used to compare three or more nouns. We usually add **-est** to the adjective or use the word **most** before it.

*Example:* The elephant is the **biggest** animal.

---

## 6. FORMING COMPARATIVE AND SUPERLATIVE DEGREES

### A. Regular Adjectives (Adding -er and -est)

For most short adjectives (one or two syllables), we add **-er** for the comparative degree and **-est** for the superlative degree.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| tall | taller | tallest |
| fast | faster | fastest |
| kind | kinder | kindest |
| small | smaller | smallest |
| long | longer | longest |

### B. Spelling Rules for -er and -est

Sometimes, we need to change the spelling of the adjective before adding -er or -est.

| Rule | Example | Comparative | Superlative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| If the adjective ends in **-e**, just add **-r** and **-st**. | large | larger | largest |
| | wise | wiser | wisest |
| If the adjective ends in a **consonant + y**, change **y** to **i** and add **-er** and **-est**. | happy | happier | happiest |
| | busy | busier | busiest |
| If the adjective ends in a **single vowel + single consonant**, double the final consonant. | big | bigger | biggest |
| | hot | hotter | hottest |

### C. Long Adjectives (Using 'more' and 'most')

For longer adjectives (usually with two or more syllables), we do not add -er or -est. Instead, we use **more** for the comparative and **most** for the superlative.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| beautiful | more beautiful | most beautiful |
| difficult | more difficult | most difficult |
| important | more important | most important |

### D. Irregular Adjectives

Some adjectives do not follow any rules. They change completely. You have to remember them!

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| good | better | best |
| bad | worse | worst |
| little | less | least |
| much / many | more | most |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |

## 7. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Ms. Sharma:** "Let's try to change these adjectives into their other forms."

**Adjective:** `happy`

1.  **Think about the rule:** The word ends in a consonant + y.
2.  **Apply the rule:** Change 'y' to 'i' and add -er/-est.
3.  **Forms:** happy, happ**ier**, happ**iest**.

**Adjective:** `beautiful`

1.  **Think about the rule:** This is a long word.
2.  **Apply the rule:** Use 'more' and 'most'.
3.  **Forms:** beautiful, **more** beautiful, **most** beautiful.

**Adjective:** `good`

1.  **Think about the rule:** This is an irregular adjective.
2.  **Apply the rule:** It has special forms.
3.  **Forms:** good, **better**, **best**.

## 8. REMEMBER! (USING COMPARISONS)

When you use comparative and superlative adjectives, look for these clues!

-   The **Comparative** form is often followed by the word **than**.
    *   *Example:* Rohan is taller **than** Maya.

-   The **Superlative** form is often used with the word **the** before it.
    *   *Example:* Rohan is **the** tallest boy in the class.

## 12. LET'S SUMMARISE

In this chapter, we learned how to compare things using adjectives.

-   **Positive Degree:** Describes one thing. (e.g., a **fast** car)
-   **Comparative Degree:** Compares two things. (e.g., a **faster** car)
-   **Superlative Degree:** Compares three or more things. (e.g., the **fastest** car)

> **Key Idea:** We change adjectives to compare things, using **-er/-est** or **more/most**. Some adjectives have special forms!
