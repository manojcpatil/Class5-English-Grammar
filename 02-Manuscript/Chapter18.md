## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
After exploring synonyms and antonyms, learners now examine a trickier vocabulary challenge: words that sound exactly the same but are spelled differently and mean different things. This is one of the most common sources of spelling errors in young writers, so this chapter gives learners clear strategies for choosing the right spelling based on meaning.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Define homophone and give examples.
- Choose the correct homophone to complete a sentence based on meaning.
- Distinguish between commonly confused pairs (their/there/they're, to/too/two, its/it's).
- Edit a sentence to correct a homophone error.

## 8. GRAMMAR WORD

## Grammar Word: HOMOPHONE

A **homophone** is a word that sounds exactly the same as another word but has a different spelling and a different meaning.

### Examples

- flower / flour — "The **flower** bloomed." vs. "We baked bread with **flour**."
- write / right — "I will **write** a letter." vs. "Turn **right** at the corner."

### Non-examples / Contrast

- "big" and "small" sound completely different and mean opposite things — they are antonyms, not homophones.

### Quick Check

What is the homophone of "sea" (a body of water)? *("see" — to look at something)*

---

## 9. CORE EXPLANATION

**to / too / two**
→ Example: "I am going **to** school." (direction/purpose)
→ Contrast: "I am **too** tired to walk." (meaning "excessively") / "I have **two** pencils." (the number 2)
→ Check: Am I showing direction (to), excess/also (too), or a number (two)?

**their / there / they're**
→ Example: "That is **their** house." (belonging to them)
→ Contrast: "Put it over **there**." (a place) / "**They're** coming to the party." (they are)
→ Check: Does the word show ownership (their), a place (there), or is it short for "they are" (they're)?

**its / it's**
→ Example: "The dog wagged **its** tail." (belonging to it)
→ Contrast: "**It's** a sunny day." (it is)
→ Check: Can I replace it with "it is"? If yes, use "it's." If it shows ownership, use "its."

---

## 10. TYPES / CLASSIFICATIONS

### Common Homophone Pairs

Name: Everyday homophone pairs
Meaning: Frequently confused pairs beyond their/there/they're and to/too/two.
Example: hear/here, write/right, know/no, buy/by/bye, sun/son, eye/I, blue/blew
Contrast: Each word in the pair has a completely different meaning, even though pronunciation is identical.
Quick Check: Which one means "not any": "know" or "no"? *(no)*

### Strategy for Choosing the Right Homophone

Name: Meaning-first strategy
Meaning: Decide the meaning you want first, then choose the spelling that matches that meaning.
Example: To say something belongs to a group of people, think "ownership" → their.
Contrast: Choosing by sound alone often leads to the wrong spelling.
Quick Check: If you mean "also," which spelling do you choose: to, too, or two? *(too)*

---

## 11. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Sentence to correct:** "There going too visit they're grandmother, who lives over their."

Step 1: Find each homophone and decide its intended meaning.

Step 2: "There going" should mean "they are going" → **they're** going.

Step 3: "too visit" should mean "in order to visit" → **to** visit.

Step 4: "they're grandmother" should show ownership → **their** grandmother.

Step 5: "over their" should mean a place → over **there**.

Answer: "**They're** going **to** visit **their** grandmother, who lives over **there**."

Why: Each homophone was chosen based on its intended meaning, not just its sound.

---

## 12. COMPARE AND NOTICE

**Incorrect:** "Its raining, so bring you're umbrella too the bus stop."
**Correct:** "**It's** raining, so bring **your** umbrella **to** the bus stop."

What changed? "Its" became "it's" (it is), "you're" became "your" (possessive — note: "your/you're" is another common confusion pair worth noticing here), and "too" became "to" (direction).
Why? Each correction matches the spelling to its intended meaning.

## 16. COMMON CONFUSION

### WATCH OUT!

Common confusion: Mixing up **their/there/they're** and **to/too/two**, since all three words in each set sound identical.

How to check: For "their/there/they're," ask: does it show ownership (their), a place (there), or can I replace it with "they are" (they're)? For "to/too/two," ask: is it showing direction/purpose (to), meaning "also/excessively" (too), or the number 2 (two)?

Correct examples: "Their bags are heavy." / "The bags are over there." / "They're coming soon." / "I went to school." / "Me too!" / "I have two erasers."

## 30. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students often choose a homophone spelling based on how frequently they have seen it, rather than checking its meaning in context, leading to persistent errors even after correction.

Support strategy: Use a consistent meaning-first questioning routine ("What do I mean here?") before writing any homophone, and keep a visible classroom reference chart.

Extension: Introduce a few additional pairs (buy/by/bye, hear/here, write/right) once the core sets are secure.

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to write a short paragraph deliberately using every homophone from the chapter correctly.
