# CHAPTER 21: ACTIVE AND PASSIVE VOICE

**Unit:** 7 — Sentence Craft

## 1. PEDAGOGICAL PURPOSE

**Why is this concept being taught here?**
Having learned to build fuller, more skilful sentences in Chapter 20, learners are ready to see that the same event can be described in two different ways, depending on whether the sentence focuses on the doer or the receiver of the action. This is an early, carefully limited introduction to voice — the goal is recognition and simple transformation, not full mastery of every passive form.

**What should the learner be able to do with it?**
The learner can:
- Identify the doer and the receiver of an action in a sentence.
- Recognise whether a sentence is in the active or passive voice.
- Change a simple active sentence into the passive voice, and back again.
- Notice when passive voice is used in real life (e.g., notices, news headlines).

## 7. DISCOVER / EXPLICIT TEACHING

### Explicit explanation

Every action has two possible "starting points" for a sentence:

- The **doer** — the person or thing that performs the action.
- The **receiver** — the person or thing that the action happens to.

| Voice | Focus | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Active Voice** | The doer is the subject. | **Rohan** kicked the ball. |
| **Passive Voice** | The receiver is the subject. | The ball **was kicked** by Rohan. |

---

## 8. GRAMMAR WORD

## Grammar Word: ACTIVE VOICE AND PASSIVE VOICE

**Active voice** is when the subject of the sentence performs the action.
**Passive voice** is when the subject of the sentence receives the action.

### Examples

- Active: The chef **cooked** the meal.
- Passive: The meal **was cooked** by the chef.

### Non-examples / Contrast

- "The meal cooked the chef" is not a correct passive of the first sentence — it reverses the actual doer and receiver, which changes the meaning entirely.

### Quick Check

In "The letter was posted by Maya," who is the receiver, and who is the doer?
*(Receiver: the letter. Doer: Maya.)*

---

## 9. CORE EXPLANATION

**Active voice — doer first**
→ Example: **The dog** chased the ball.
→ Contrast: The ball was chased **by the dog**. (same event, passive voice)
→ Check: Is the subject of the sentence the one doing the action? Then it is active.

**Passive voice — receiver first**
→ Example: **The ball** was chased by the dog.
→ Contrast: The dog chased **the ball**. (same event, active voice)
→ Check: Is the subject of the sentence the one the action happens to? Then it is passive.

**Forming the passive (simple present and simple past only)**
→ Example (present): Maya **writes** the letter. → The letter **is written** by Maya.
→ Example (past): Maya **wrote** the letter. → The letter **was written** by Maya.
→ Contrast: The passive always uses a form of "be" (is/am/are/was/were) plus the past participle of the main verb (written, cooked, broken, cleaned).
→ Check: Have I used the correct form of "be" to match the new subject, and the past participle of the verb?

---

## 10. TYPES / CLASSIFICATIONS

### Doer and Receiver

Name: Doer and receiver
Meaning: The doer performs the action; the receiver is affected by the action.
Example: "The baker baked the bread." Doer: the baker. Receiver: the bread.
Contrast: In the passive version, "The bread was baked by the baker," the receiver becomes the subject, but the doer and receiver do not change roles — only their position in the sentence changes.
Quick Check: In "The teacher checked the homework," who is the doer and who is the receiver?

### The "by" Phrase

Name: The "by" phrase
Meaning: In a passive sentence, "by + doer" tells us who performed the action. It can sometimes be left out if the doer is unknown or unimportant.
Example: "The window was broken **by the storm**." / "The window was broken." (doer not mentioned)
Contrast: Active sentences never need a "by" phrase, because the doer is already the subject.
Quick Check: Is a "by" phrase always necessary in a passive sentence? *(No — only when we want to say who did it.)*

---

## 11. WORKED EXAMPLE

**Sentence to transform:** "The children ate the cake." (Change to passive voice.)

Step 1: Identify the doer and the receiver. Doer: the children. Receiver: the cake.

Step 2: Make the receiver the new subject: "The cake..."

Step 3: Choose the correct form of "be" to match the tense (past) and the new subject (singular): "was."

Step 4: Add the past participle of the main verb: "eat" → "eaten."

Step 5: Add "by + doer" if needed: "by the children."

Answer: "**The cake was eaten by the children.**"

Why: The receiver ("the cake") becomes the subject, "was" matches the past tense and singular subject, and "eaten" is the correct past participle of "eat."

---

## 12. COMPARE AND NOTICE

**Active:** The gardener waters the plants every day.
**Passive:** The plants are watered every day (by the gardener).

What changed? The receiver ("the plants") became the subject, and the verb changed from "waters" to "are watered."
Why? The passive version shifts the focus from who does the watering to what happens to the plants — useful when the doer is obvious, unknown, or unimportant.

## 16. COMMON CONFUSION

### WATCH OUT!

Common confusion: Learners often use the wrong form of the main verb in the passive (for example, "was eat" or "was breaking" instead of "was eaten" or "was broken"), because they forget that the passive needs the **past participle**, not the base form or the "-ing" form.

How to check: Ask, "Have I used 'be' (is/am/are/was/were) followed by the special past-participle form of the verb (eaten, broken, written), not the plain verb or the '-ing' form?"

Correct examples: "The window was broken" (not "was break" or "was breaking"). "The letter is written" (not "is write" or "is writing").

## 17. LANGUAGE IN REAL LIFE

- **News headline:** "Two students **were awarded** prizes for their science project." (doer not important)
- **Notice board:** "Visitors **are requested** to switch off their phones." (polite, doer understood as "everyone")
- **Recipe:** "The vegetables **are chopped** and then **added** to the pan." (focus on the process, not the cook)
- **Story:** "The treasure **was found** by two brave explorers." (focus on the treasure and the exciting discovery)

## 25. SUMMARY

- **Active voice** focuses on the doer of an action; the doer is the subject.
- **Passive voice** focuses on the receiver of an action; the receiver is the subject.
- The passive voice is formed with a form of "be" (is/am/are/was/were) plus the past participle of the main verb.
- A "by" phrase can name the doer in a passive sentence, but it can also be left out.
- Passive voice is useful when the doer is unknown, unimportant, or already understood.

---

## 26. KEY TERMS

| Term | Simple Meaning |
|---|---|
| Active voice | The subject performs the action |
| Passive voice | The subject receives the action |
| Doer | The person or thing performing the action |
| Receiver | The person or thing the action happens to |
| Past participle | The special verb form used after "be" in the passive (eaten, broken, written) |

## 30. TEACHER SUPPORT NOTE

Likely misconception: Students often use the wrong verb form after "be" (e.g., "was eat," "was breaking") because they confuse the passive with the continuous tense, or they attempt to passivise sentences with no clear receiver (e.g., "It was rained").

Support strategy: Restrict practice at this level to simple, clearly transitive sentences (sentences with an obvious doer and receiver) in the simple present and simple past only. Avoid intransitive verbs (like "rain," "arrive," "sleep") in passive practice, since they cannot be passivised.

Extension: Briefly mention that not every sentence can be made passive (e.g., "The bird sleeps" has no receiver to become the new subject).

Challenge: Ask advanced learners to find a short passage and identify which sentences could be rewritten in the other voice, and whether doing so would change the meaning or emphasis.
