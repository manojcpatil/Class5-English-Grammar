\chapter{Revision and Practice Tests: Showing What You Know}

\section{Pedagogical Purpose}
This is the final chapter of the book. Its purpose is to offer learners a chance to show what they know, independently, in a format close to a real class test. It consolidates the whole book through balanced, mixed-format practice tests covering grammar, vocabulary, sentence construction, editing, and writing together.

\begin{learningobjectives}
The learner can:
\begin{itemize}
    \item complete a full mixed-section practice test independently.
    \item use test-taking strategies like reading instructions carefully and checking answers.
    \item mark their own or a partner's completed test against a full answer key.
    \item identify, from their own results, one or two areas that would benefit from further revision.
\end{itemize}
\end{learningobjectives}

\section{Prerequisite Check}
\begin{quickcheck}
Before beginning, look back at the Key Terms tables in Chapters 13-28. Choose any three terms and, without checking the definitions, try to explain each one in your own words.
\end{quickcheck}

\begin{rememberbox}
This chapter does not teach anything new. Everything in the two practice tests that follow has already been taught somewhere earlier in this book. If a question feels unfamiliar, that is a signal to revisit the relevant chapter afterwards.
\end{rememberbox}

\section{Chapter Opener}
It is the last week of term, and Ms. Sharma hands out two papers, face down, to the whole class.

\textbf{Ms. Sharma:} "Today and next week, we are going to do something a little different. These papers cover everything we have learned this year. I want you to treat this like a real test: read every instruction carefully, manage your time, and check your work before you finish."

\textbf{Rohan:} \textit{(a little nervous)} "What if I get something wrong?"

\textbf{Ms. Sharma:} "Then you'll know exactly what to revise before the real exam — which is the whole point! A practice test is not there to catch you out. It's there to show you what you already know well and what still needs a little more practice."

\textbf{Maya:} "That actually makes it feel less scary."

\section{Test-Taking Strategy}
Before beginning either practice test, keep these strategies in mind:

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{|l|p{8cm}|}
\hline
\textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Why It Helps} \\
\hline
Read every instruction fully & Some sections ask for more than one thing (e.g., "correct the sentence and explain why"). \\
Attempt every section & A partial answer usually earns more than a blank one. \\
Watch the time & Spending too long on one section can leave too little time for others. \\
Check your work at the end & Many small errors are easy to catch on a second look. \\
If stuck, move on and return later & Getting stuck on one question can cost time you need elsewhere. \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\section{Practice Test A}
\textbf{Suggested time: 60 minutes. Total marks: 50.}

\subsection*{Section 1 — Grammar Identification (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Underline the noun and circle the verb: \textit{The children played happily in the garden.} (2 marks)
    \item What tense is this sentence? \textit{"Maya will visit her cousin next week."} (1 mark)
    \item Identify the adjective and the noun it describes: \textit{The clever fox tricked the crow.} (2 marks)
    \item Is the underlined verb an action verb or a verb of being? \textit{Leo \textbf{is} a clever parrot.} (1 mark)
    \item Identify the conjunction and explain what relationship it shows: \textit{"I wanted to play outside, but it started raining."} (2 marks)
    \item Choose the correct article: \textit{I saw \_\_\_ (a/an) elephant at the zoo.} (1 mark)
    \item Is this sentence active or passive? \textit{"The cake was baked by Maya."} (1 mark)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Section 2 — Vocabulary (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Give one synonym and one antonym for "happy." (2 marks)
    \item Fill in the correct homophone: \textit{\_\_\_ (Their/There/They're) going to the market.} (1 mark)
    \item Fill in the correct homophone: \textit{\_\_\_ (Its/It's) raining today.} (1 mark)
    \item Add the prefix "un-" to "kind" and explain the new word's meaning. (2 marks)
    \item Add the suffix "-ful" to "care" and use the new word in a sentence of your own. (2 marks)
    \item Rewrite this sentence, replacing the vague word with a more precise one: \textit{"The food was nice."} (2 marks)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Section 3 — Subject-Verb Agreement and Tense (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Choose the correct verb: \textit{The basket of apples (is/are) heavy.} (1 mark)
    \item Correct this sentence: \textit{"The children plays outside every afternoon."} (2 marks)
    \item Correct this sentence: \textit{"Yesterday, we go to the fair and we eated ice cream."} (3 marks)
    \item Complete with the correct present continuous form: \textit{Look! The dog \_\_\_ (bark) at the gate.} (1 mark)
    \item Rewrite this sentence in the future tense: \textit{"Leo eats seeds."} (1 mark)
    \item Correct the tense-consistency error: \textit{"Every day, Maya feeds her fish. Yesterday, she cleans the tank."} (2 marks)
\end{enumerate}

\section{Practice Test B}
\textbf{Suggested time: 60 minutes. Total marks: 50.}

\subsection*{Section 1 — Grammar Identification (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Underline the subject and circle the predicate: \textit{The old clock struck thirteen.} (2 marks)
    \item Identify the preposition and its kind (place, time, or direction): \textit{"The meeting will start at 10 o'clock."} (2 marks)
    \item Identify the pronoun and say whether it is a subject or object pronoun: \textit{"Rohan is waiting for us."} (2 marks)
    \item What kind of sentence is this? \textit{"What a wonderful surprise!"} (1 mark)
    \item Identify the adverb and the word it describes: \textit{"She sang beautifully."} (2 marks)
    \item Change this active sentence into the passive voice: \textit{"The teacher checked our homework."} (1 mark)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Section 2 — Vocabulary (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Sort these words into three pairs, and say whether each pair is a synonym pair or an antonym pair: \textit{happy, sad, joyful, tiny, huge, cheerful.} (3 marks)
    \item Fill in the correct homophone: \textit{I have \_\_\_ (to/too/two) pencils.} (1 mark)
    \item What one word means "a person who cannot hear"? (1 mark)
    \item Explain the difference in meaning between "careful" and "careless." (2 marks)
    \item Rewrite this sentence to remove the repeated word: \textit{"The tall boy climbed the tall tree to see the tall building."} (3 marks)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Section 3 — Sentence Construction and Transformation (10 marks)}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Combine these two sentences using a suitable joining word: \textit{"It was raining. We stayed inside."} (2 marks)
    \item Change this sentence from direct to reported speech: \textit{Maya said, "I am tired."} (2 marks)
    \item Change this sentence from reported to direct speech, adding correct punctuation: \textit{Rohan said that he was hungry.} (2 marks)
    \item Expand this simple sentence with two details (when and where): \textit{"The children laughed."} (2 marks)
    \item Fix the word order: \textit{"Quickly the girl the ball threw."} (2 marks)
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Section 5 — Writing (10 marks)}
Choose \textbf{one}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Write a short informal letter (6-8 lines) to a friend, telling them about something exciting that happened recently.
    \item Write a short story completion (5-7 sentences) continuing this opener: \textit{"The old clock in the hallway struck thirteen, and that was when everything changed."}
\end{itemize}
